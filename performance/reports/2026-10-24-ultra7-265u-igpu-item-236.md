# Where the metropolis's draw commands go, for item 236, 2026-10-24

Measured for [item 236](../../roadmap/236-where-a-vehicle-stands-changes-a-frames-draw-count.md), whose ceilings in `TestTheWorkAFrameTakes` had been raised three times in one night.

**Rig:** Intel Core Ultra 7 265U, 14 threads, 62 GiB; every run under Xvfb at 1600×900 through the acceptance suite's own headless city, so no GPU drew and no figure here is a GPU's; Arch Linux, kernel 7.2.
It is the machine of [the item 157 report](2026-10-21-ultra7-265u-igpu-item-157.md).
**Builds:** r980 (`67b53eb`, item 221's raise); r992 (`5147505`, main when item 222 was held); r993 (`51c05d1`, [item 222](../../roadmap/222-cars-drive-on-the-kerb-when-zoomed-in.md), unpushed, on `5147505`).
Each was built with `go build -tags "devtools ebitenginedebug" ./cmd/botropolis`, as `TestTheWorkAFrameTakes` builds it, in a detached worktree of the performance manager's own.
Three measurement-only variants of r992 and r993 were built with `go build -overlay`, never committed: sprites with `DisableMipmaps`, a level-aware `batchKey`, and both.
A probe in `pkg/render`, also never committed, logged each draw of the mover pass with its page, filter, matrix, mipmap level and the line that recorded it.
**History on disk:** none: every run staged the demo's metropolis or hamlet into a scratch home.
**Runs:** not one of the runs in [method.md](../method.md), but `TestTheWorkAFrameTakes`'s own measurement: Ebitengine's command log over one second at 60 frames, the worst settled frame from the first frame the city drew, skipping 17.
Two runs of each case, by day (14:00) and by night (22:00); each pair agreed.
Every run held `/tmp/botropolis-land.lock`.

## Findings

1. **The metropolis's settled frame is cheap; its movers are not.** With `BOTROPOLIS_FRAME_MOVERS=none`, the metropolis issues 9 commands by day and 14 by night, as the hamlet does; with its movers, 26 and 43 at r992 (counts table).
   Almost all of the difference is the pass that redraws each rectangle a mover is in, from the ground up, with every building it overlaps, in the order `batch()` in `pkg/render/dirtystream.go` chooses.
2. **One atlas page is four textures to Ebitengine, and `batchKey` sees one.** `drawSprite` (`pkg/render/iso.go`) draws every kit sprite with `FilterLinear` and mipmaps on.
   Shrunk below half size, Ebitengine samples a mipmap level of the page, and each level is its own texture: in r980's command log one page is sources 10, 17 and 20.
   A colour-matrix draw goes through `DrawRectShader`, which never uses a mipmap, so [item 221](../../roadmap/221-the-railway-is-drawn-over-the-trees.md)'s trees read source 2, the page itself.
   `batchKey` keys on the page pointer, so `batch()` groups draws Ebitengine cannot merge, and which levels a mover's rectangle pulls in decides the count: the ratchet the item names.
3. **A level-aware `batchKey` alone puts item 222 under the frozen ceilings**: 27/44 → 24/41, and main 26/43 → 23/40 (counts table).
   It reorders only draws that do not overlap, and slice 1 of item 236 changed no pixels in the frames checked (pixel check).
4. **Sprites without mipmaps remove the ratchet**: r992 and r993 both give 21/38, so item 222's vehicles cost nothing (counts table).
   It changes how sprites look below half size: at fit, 36 pixels differ in a 1600×900 frame; at zoom 0.35, 0.81% do, as harder edges on rooftop details and trim.
   Stills cannot show shimmer while panning, so this one is Aria's to watch.
5. **Sprites without mipmaps save 5.2 MiB of texture, and none settled at full zoom.**
   The saving is three textures, levels 1–3 of z1's first page (2048×2046), built at fit; no level of z1's second page or of any z1.5 page is built at any zoom up to 2.0 (texture census).
   They stay while z1 does, and `Mipmap.Deallocate` frees them with it, so once the kits let z1 go at full zoom the saving is 0.
   It does not close [item 162](../../roadmap/162-the-city-holds-gigabytes-of-gpu-memory.md)'s gap at full zoom.
   **An earlier bound of about 18 MiB, sent while this was measured, was wrong**: it assumed every page builds a full mipmap chain, a third on top of each, when Ebitengine builds a level only for a linear draw shrunk below half size and a level only on the page drawn that way.
   Bound a texture cost from what is allocated, not from what could be.
6. **Every build still varies by one command between settled frames** (each count is a pair, *min–max*), so something else still moves with where vehicles stand.
   That is the next thing to find, and not item 236's first slice.

## Pixel check

Slice 1 of item 236 at `83a1147`, a level-aware `batchKey` on Ebitengine's own per-edge rule, against r992, by day at fit and at 0.35: every recorded frame is identical to the bit, six frames at each zoom.

## After slice 2: the fog, and the night's last commands

Measured at r1013 (`8168db2`, slice 2: kit sprites without mipmaps, ceilings 21 / 37), over four seconds of frames rather than the test's one, so 104 settled frames a run instead of about 14; two runs of each case, identical; through `make test-acceptance-locked`.

1. **The fog costs nothing.** A variant without [item 239](../../roadmap/239-the-bridge-fades-into-no-visible-fog.md)'s fog, measurement only, reads 20–22 by day and 36–40 by night, no fewer than with it (20–21, 36–40), so its puffs' mipmap levels split no batch where movers cross them.
2. **By night the count is 36 in 6 settled frames, 37 in 62, and 40 in 36**, three over the ceiling.
   The 40s are one unbroken run of 36 frames, from 20 frames after the test's settled cut, at the same place in both runs: cars passing lit buildings.
   `TestTheWorkAFrameTakes` records one second, and its window ends about 6 frames before that run, so it passes; a city that draws its first frame sooner would read 40 and fail.
3. **The cause is the lights stream.** In a mover's rectangle, `layLightsStream` (`pkg/render/stillframe.go`) replays each lit window in depth order, laying the cut-outs due before it (destination-out) and then its pane (plain, or warm glass through a colour matrix), so panes and cut-outs alternate: the lights layer takes 13 commands in a 37-command frame and 16 in a 40-command one.
   About five (source, shader, blend) keys are in play, so batching the lights stream as `batch()` batches the mover pass could save up to about 8 commands at 37 and 11 at 40; fewer where a cut-out must stay after a pane it overlaps, which only a build counts.
4. **By day the count is 20 in the first 6 settled frames and 21 after**, one linear sprite draw from a mover entering a rectangle, inside the ceiling.

## What it does not show

- **A GPU.** Every figure is Xvfb's, so the census is what Ebitengine allocates, not what a driver holds; one `perf-fitzoom` pass with and without sprite mipmaps would confirm it on the real display, which needs Aria's screen.
- **The 0.25 zoom step.** The metropolis fits at 0.267 at 1600×900 and the city does not zoom out past fit, so the stills are at fit and 0.35.
- **Shimmer.** Stills show the edges of a still frame, not how they move while panning.
- **CPU.** Fewer commands should cost less CPU a frame; nothing here measured it.
- **What moves the last command.** Finding 6.

## Reproduce

The runs used scratch harnesses, kept with the raw runs under `~/.local/state/botropolis-perf/` on the measuring machine, not in any repository.
The cut they report is the one `TestTheWorkAFrameTakes` logs under `-v`:

```sh
make -C botropolis test-acceptance-locked ARGS='-run TestTheWorkAFrameTakes -v'
```

# 0.2.2 against 0.1.7 on a second integrated-graphics laptop, 2026-10-19

**Rig:** Intel Core Ultra 7 265U, 14 threads, 62 GiB, 4 GiB swap; the city draws on the **integrated Arrow Lake-U GPU** (i915), the only GPU in the machine; 1920×1200, i3 4.25 on X11, Arch Linux, kernel 7.2.
Like [the first laptop](2026-10-19-laptop-igpu-0.2.2.md) it has far more RAM than the reference machine in [budgets.md](../budgets.md), so every figure is judged against the budgets, not against what this machine can absorb.
**Builds:** 0.2.2 (r745, `29b18c5`) as packaged (`0.2.2-1`), and built with a pprof endpoint by `perf-pprof-build`; 0.1.7 (r409, `62c50ee`) the same way; HEAD at r780 (`1db46d9`, the code of r779 plus frames) the same way; go 1.27.2.
**History on disk:** 122 transcripts, 2.4 GiB, 105 projects, 8 written in the last hour, about 20 `claude` processes running.
**Runs:** daemon at rest (600 s at 1 s for 0.1.7, 900 s for 0.2.2 after a restart), daemon A/B (300 s at 1 s), cold start (until idle, at 1 s), cold start interrupted and resumed, the usage probe by hand, and the city on the real display (900 s, hidden at 600) for 0.2.2, r780 and 0.1.7, plus 0.2.2 and r780 headless; between 17:24 and 18:41 MDT.

## Before reading the numbers

- **The production daemon on this machine was still 0.1.7.** The package went from 0.1.7 to 0.2.2 at 09:51, but `botropolisd` had run since 2026-10-17 from the deleted 0.1.7 binary, because the install scriptlet's `systemctl --user enable --now` does not restart a running service.
  So the first *at rest* row is 0.1.7; Aria restarted the daemon at 18:25, and the 0.2.2 row after it was taken then.
  0.2.3 makes a mismatched city and daemon visible (item 152), which is the same situation.
- **Every daemon copy began on a store with nothing real in it.** The real `stats.db` held only rows written by a test (filed as item 165), so the A/B copies of the state directory started almost cold, and their first half-minute is a first launch, not a settled daemon.
  The A/B rows below are split into start-up and settled for that reason.
- **The 0.2.2 and r780 cities were never focused, so they drew no frames.** Someone was using the desktop; the 0.2.2 window was focused once by hand and focus returned to them within seconds, and this report did not fight for it.
  Their memory and GPU are what a visible, unfocused city holds, and **no 0.2.2 frame time was measured on this rig**.
  The 0.1.7 window happened to get focus when it opened, and drew 840 frames.

## Findings

Most expensive first, each against [budgets.md](../budgets.md).

1. **The city's own memory and GPU together reach 3.76 GB about four seconds after start-up in 0.2.2, and settle at 1.94 GB** (run: city, 0.2.2). Budget: ≤ 1 GB at start-up, ≤ 512 MiB settled.
   The Go heap peaks at 2.3 GB and keeps 798 MB live.
   At r780 the GPU share is lower (668 MiB against 988, item 70's own textures), but the live heap is **higher, 1.21 GB**, so the total is the same 1.96 GB.
2. **85% of r780's 1.2 GB heap is Ebitengine's draw-command queue, filled through the `vector` package** (heap profile, real display, 3 s and 60 s).
   The stack is `DrawImage` → `invokeUsageCallbacks` → `vector.fillPathCallback` → `fillPaths` → `EnqueueDrawTrianglesCommand`: `vector` defers fills and strokes and flushes them as triangles when an image they touch is next drawn.
   So `kitRising`/`kitAt` (542 MB), `isoTree` (294 MB) and `vector.FillRect` (236 MB) are **where the deferred fills were flushed, not where they were issued**, which corrects the first laptop report's reading of the same profile as sprites.
   `vector` fill and stroke call sites in `pkg/render` went from 72 in 0.1.7 to 161 in 0.2.2, on Ebitengine v2.10.2 throughout.
3. **A hidden city keeps all of it**: 933 MiB own + 988 MiB GPU five minutes after hiding in 0.2.2, and 1,293 + 668 MiB at r780 (run: city, hidden). Budget: own memory ≤ 256 MiB within 30 s. Its CPU is within budget, 0.5–1.0%.
4. **First launch takes 3.6 cores at its peak** (run: cold): 15 s at a mean of 212%, 32 CPU-seconds, **10,778 collections**, 3,645 MiB read for 2.4 GiB of history.
   `GOMEMLIMIT=256MiB` takes the same start to a 115% peak, 10.8 CPU-seconds and 357 collections: the extra cores are the collector against the 16 MiB cap. Budget: ≤ 1 core in all.
5. **A first launch quit part-way starts over** (run: interrupted): stopped at 6 s with 1,547 MiB read, the relaunch read 3,236 MiB more, 89% of an uninterrupted run. Budget: quitting part-way loses nothing.
6. **The usage probe, as `RefreshUtilization` runs it, peaks at 904–970 MiB across five processes**; with `--setting-sources "" --strict-mcp-config` it is one process at **408–426 MiB**, which still breaks the 300 MiB helper budget (run: probe).
   `--tools ""`, which `Summarise` also passes, swallows `/usage` as a tool name, so the probe cannot copy it as is.
7. **The 0.2.2 daemon's collector runs 16 times as often as 0.1.7's even when settled** with no hook pushes: 41 collections a minute against 2.6, and 8,344 in its first 38 s (run: A/B).
   Settled CPU is within budget here, 0.4% against 0.1% for 0.1.7, because these copies receive no hook pushes; the first laptop's production daemon, which did, averaged 35.6%.
8. **The production 0.2.2 daemon, with sessions working, averaged 17.0% of a core over fifteen minutes, p95 52%**, in 70 bursts with 32 s at or above a full core, and read 4,975 MiB, with 38 five-second windows over 25 MiB/s and a peak of 154 (run: at rest, after Aria's restart). Budgets: ≤ 2% mean, ≤ 25% p95, ≤ 1 MiB/s, ≤ 25 MiB/s over any 5 s.
   Its last three and a half minutes were quiet, at 1.6%; it is the A/B copies' 0.4% plus what hook pushes and live sessions add (items 157, 158).
   The store was not written, so this was not a first launch.
9. **The production daemon, 0.1.7, before the restart, read 4.2 GB in ten minutes**, in seven bursts of about 600 MB — the 590 MiB of transcripts written in the last hour, one of them 421 MiB — with 22 s at or above a full core and a 5-second peak of 169 MiB/s (run: at rest). Budget: ≤ 25 MiB/s over any 5 s.
   0.2.2 still re-reads a live transcript whole on each change; item 141 fixed that at r747, after 0.2.2, so this is filed nowhere new.
10. **Even 0.1.7 is four to six times over the drawing budget**: focused, it drew at 10.3 ms of CPU a frame and used **50–60% of a core** for the 32 s it drew (run: city, 0.1.7). Budget: ≤ 10 ms a frame and ≤ 10% of a core.
   So the 41 ms the first laptop measured for 0.2.2 is a regression on top of a city that was already over.
11. **While unfocused and not drawing, the city's own memory plus GPU grew 4.9 MiB a minute** from 60 to 600 s (0.2.2) and 10.1 MiB a minute at r780. Budget: ≤ 1 MiB an hour. Ten minutes is not a soak; item 164 is.

## The tools

Two defects in the tools surfaced on this rig and are fixed in the same commit as this report:

- **`perf-env` exited 1 whenever the state directory had no `sessions.json`**, because its last line was `[[ -f … ]] && …`, and `perf-daemon-ab` runs under `set -e`, so every A/B died silently before starting a daemon.
  A test now runs `perf-env` on an empty state directory.
- **`perf-city --hide-after` could never hide a window on i3**, because `[pid=…]` is sway's criterion, not i3's; and it wrote `hidden_at_s` to `env.txt` whether or not the hide worked, so a run that was never hidden read as one that was.
  It now finds the window by `_NET_WM_PID` and says `hide_failed_at_s` when it cannot; the first laptop's hidden rows are worth reading with that in mind.

And one gap is noted, not fixed: **under `--headless`, `PERF_PPROF` profiles the wrong process.**
The headless city runs as two `botropolis` processes, both inherit `PERF_PPROF`, and the outer one takes the port, so its heap profile shows a few MB while the inner one holds hundreds.
Profile on the real display, where the city is one process.

## What it does not show

- **0.2.2's frame time.** Its city drew no frames on this rig; the first report's 41 ms is still the only figure for 0.2.2.
- **Group detection's share** was not profiled on this rig; the first report's 77% stands.
- **macOS**, as before.
- **Anything longer than fifteen minutes**, so finding 9 is a lead for a soak, not a leak.

## Reproduce

From the meta repository's root, with botropolis checked out beside it; `T` is any scratch directory.

```sh
performance/tools/perf-env
make perf-daemon PERF_SECONDS=600
for r in v0.1.7 v0.2.2 HEAD; do performance/tools/perf-pprof-build $r $T/perf-$r; done
printf '#!/bin/sh\nGOMEMLIMIT=256MiB exec %s/perf-v0.2.2/botropolisd "$@"\n' $T >$T/perf-memlimit && chmod +x $T/perf-memlimit
performance/tools/perf-daemon-ab --seconds 300 --interval 1 v0.1.7=$T/perf-v0.1.7/botropolisd v0.2.2=$T/perf-v0.2.2/botropolisd memlimit=$T/perf-memlimit
performance/tools/perf-daemon-ab --cold --seconds 900 --interval 1 v0.1.7=$T/perf-v0.1.7/botropolisd v0.2.2=$T/perf-v0.2.2/botropolisd memlimit=$T/perf-memlimit
# the probe, both ways, from its own directory, sampling the tree:
(cd ~/.local/state/botropolis/usage-probe && claude -p /usage & performance/tools/perf-sample --tree $! --interval 0.5 --duration 30)
(cd ~/.local/state/botropolis/usage-probe && claude -p /usage --setting-sources "" --strict-mcp-config & performance/tools/perf-sample --tree $! --interval 0.5 --duration 30)
# the city, against a daemon of the same build on a copy of the state directory (item 152 refuses a mismatch):
performance/tools/perf-city --seconds 900 --hide-after 600 -- --socket <that daemon's socket>
performance/tools/perf-city --bin $T/perf-v0.1.7/botropolis --seconds 240
```
